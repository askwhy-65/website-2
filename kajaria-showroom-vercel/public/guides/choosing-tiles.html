A practical planning guide

How to choose tiles for your home.

A simple framework for comparing style, finish, size, maintenance and quantity before you commit to a tile.

Tile decisions affect how a room looks, feels and functions every day. Start with the space and the job the surface needs to do, then narrow the design choices. Use the checklist below as a conversation starter with your tile supplier and installer.

1. Start with the room’s purpose

A bathroom floor, kitchen backsplash, living room and outdoor path face different conditions. Consider moisture, foot traffic, sunlight, cleaning products and the likelihood of spills. Check manufacturer guidance for the exact product and intended application.

2. Choose a suitable finish

  • Matte and textured: can provide a softer visual finish; suitability for wet areas still depends on the product’s tested performance.
  • Glossy and polished: reflect more light and can create a bright, crisp look; ask about visibility of marks and slip performance.
  • Stone or marble-look: offer a natural pattern aesthetic with tile maintenance characteristics, depending on the product.
  • Wood-look: bring timber-inspired colour and grain to areas where tile may be preferred.

3. Think about tile size and layout

Larger formats may reduce the number of grout joints but need suitable substrate preparation and installation. Smaller formats can work around slopes and compact spaces. Ask your installer about layout, cuts, grout width, level changes and any required movement joints.

4. Compare colour in your actual lighting

Screen images and showroom lighting can change how colour appears. If possible, view a sample near your cabinetry, wall paint or other materials. Check multiple pieces to understand natural variation, and confirm shade and batch when ordering.

5. Estimate area and allowance

Measure the length and width of each rectangular area in feet and multiply them to estimate square footage. Split irregular rooms into smaller rectangles, add them together, then discuss a suitable cutting and breakage allowance with your installer. The website’s tile calculator can help with an initial estimate.

Before you place the order: confirm product code, dimensions, finish, technical data, shade and calibre, stock, delivery terms, return policy, quantity and any extra material for future repairs.

6. Ask practical questions before buying

  • Is this exact product recommended for the intended floor or wall application?
  • What is the manufacturer’s slip-resistance information for this finish?
  • Are all boxes from the same shade and calibre batch?
  • What installation materials and preparation are recommended?
  • What is the lead time and what happens if additional boxes are needed?

Need help making a shortlist?

Share your room dimensions, preferred look and approximate budget with the showroom. You do not need every answer before you start — the goal is to ask better questions and compare options with confidence.
